Asha sings on  
 Posted by  jay   jay@123ofnet.com

If there is one singer who could sing sensuous numbers like "Piya Tu Ab To Aaja" from 'Caravan' and "Raat Akeli Hai" from 'Jewel Thief' and the masterly ghazal compositions in 'Umrao Jaan' like "Dil Cheez Kya Hai" and "In Aankhon Ke Masti" with proficiency, then it has to be Asha Bhosle.
Talking about her renditions in ‘Umrao Jaan’, Asha says, "I was skeptical about the results when (O.P.) Khayyam lowered my pitch by half a note. But I have always believed that the composer calls the tune, so I sang just as (O.P.) Khayyam wanted me to do."

Sister of the most influential voice of India, Lata Mangeshkar, Asha Bhosle was born September 8, 1933. In April 1942, her father Dinath Mangeshkar died, causing upheaval in the family which moved from Pune to Kolhapur and in turn to Bombay. Around the age of ten she apparently sang her first film song in the Marathi film 'Majha Bal'. Asha Bhosle has since sung in virtually every Indian language, in Russian and Malay, has sung Rabindra Sangeet (the songs of Bengali poet Rabindra Tagore), has sung with the bhangra group Alaap, the Indian rap act Baba Sehgal, boyband Code Red and Boy George, the former lead vocalist of the British pop group Culture Club. Britpop act Cornershop even dedicated a song to her, named "Brimful Of Asha." It was an international hit, and it was remixed by Fatboy Slim too.

Asha was undoubtedly at her best during the mid 80’s and it was then that she rendered "Mera Kuch Saaman Tumhaare Paas Pada Hai," the R.D. Burman composition for Gulzar's 'Ijaazat' that won her the National Award as Best Singer. At this point, there were quite a few who felt that Asha was not just Lata's peer, but had forged ahead of her and it had taken Asha the best part of 35 years to achieve this.

It all began when Asha met with O.P. Nayyar. O.P. Nayyar was one composer who first gave Asha her own distinct performing identity. Their partnership made for memorable music. "Jaadoogar Sanwariya" (Dhake ki Malmal), "Chhota Sa Baalama" (Raagini), "Aankhon Se Jo Uttari Hai Dil Mein' (Phir Wohi Dil Laya Hoon), "Bekasi Hadse Jab Guzar Jaaye" (Kalpana), "Akeli Hoon Main Piya Aa Aa Aa" (Sambandh) and last but not the least, "Chainse Hum Ko Kabhi" (Pran Jaye Par Vachan Na Jaye) are but some of the Nayyar tunes that rank as musical landmarks in Asha's life. Even the "Dum Maaro Dum" effect that Asha went on to achieve, under the baton of R.D. Burman, could not erase the sultry-sirenish garb in which Nayyar had casted her in.

There was a lot said and written about the Asha-O.P. partnership. Many tend to forget that she also sang number after magnificent number under S.D. Burman, in the same era. Probably people had things to say because Nayyar was the one and only composer for whom there was no Lata, only Asha. Even though O.P. Nayyar and S.D. Burman vied with each other to exploit Asha's vocals to the hilt, she remained second choice with all other composers who mattered in 1951-60, the golden decade of Hindustani film music. It is not as though, in this decade, Asha did not give splendid results under composers mentally committed to Lata.

Review of 'Mann ke manjeere'  
 Posted by  bhavin   bhavin@123ofnet.com

This album has been conceived and produced by Breakthrough, an organisation working towards increasing public dialogue and awareness about human rights and social justice issues through education and popular culture. Mann ke manjeere is an attempt to make people aware of women's hopes and desires and helps to make them come true. Each song has been crafted to speak of women's dreams. As one listens to the songs, there is an awareness of the hopes and ambitions that women harbour but cannot articulate.

Most songs in the album have been sung by Shubha Mudgal. This is her fourth album after Ali more angna, Ab ke saawan and Pyar ke geet. This album starts off with Maati (Earth), by Shubha. The song speaks of the relationship between women, their work and the Earth. It is a tribute to the roles played by a woman as a provider and protector and is dedicated to the work of rural women, which more often than not goes unsung.

The next Mann ke manjeere was actually responsible for my buying the album, captivated as I was with its video directed by Sujit Sircar and Gary and featuring the actress, Mita Vashisht. The song portrays a woman who has embarked on the path of self-discovery. She celebrates with all the elements of nature the discovery of her own vision and voice.

Babul jiya more is an adaptation of a traditional thumri by Shubha. It is about a girl telling her father on the kind of man she wants to marry. She asks not to be married to a jeweller or a businessman or a king as she does not fancy jewels or money and neither does she want to rule; she wants to be wed to an ironsmith who will melt her chains.

Mahalakshmi Iyer, who has sung many songs for A.R. Rahman, sings Beeti raina, a contemporary song about a woman coming into her own. Jheel, by Shubha, compares a woman to a lake which is surrounded by land and therefore, is contained. The woman, like the lake, wants to break her barriers and flow freely and chart new paths.

Rukmabai, a traditional Rajasthani singer, sings Kesariya Balam, a folk song. Sung in Raag Mand, it describes a woman's call to her lover to return safely from war. Khwab, khwab has been sung by Antara Chowdhury, daughter of the renowned composer and lyricist, SalilChowdhury. It is about a girl who wants to turn her dreams into reality.

Aiva, aiva is an old folk song from the Kharwa fishing community in Saurashtra. More than 200 years old, it is about a woman waiting for her husband to return form the sea. Diwaliben Bhil, a renowned folk singer from Junagadh, has sung the song. Intezaar, by Shubha again, describes the eternal wait of a woman, waiting for her dreams to be realized and acknowledged.

Bowie 'most inspiring' musician  
 Posted by  varma   love4vishal@rediffmail.com

David Bowie has been voted the biggest influence on the current generation of musicians in a survey of rock and pop stars.
The original glam rocker beat Radiohead - who came in second - and The Beatles - who came in third - in the Under the Influence poll by the music paper NME.

Others included in the top 10 most musically or lyrically inspirational acts were the Sex Pistols and The Smiths.



Among the musicians taking part were Radiohead, Placebo, U2, Suede and Marilyn Manson.Bowie, 52, stretched rock music's boundaries in the 70s with his hits Space Oddity, Changes and Suffragette City.

He has continued to have varying degrees of chart success with tracks in the 80s and 90s such as Let's Dance and Dead Man Walking.

Many of his songs have been covered by modern bands such as Nirvana and Oasis.

Among the musicians who voted for him were groups Radiohead, Suede and U2 and shock rocker Marilyn Manson.

"Today there seems to be a lack of icons and rock stars in general," said Manson.

"When I grew up, there was David Bowie and Iggy Pop - people who had something to say and had quite an impact on music and society," he added.

Runners-up Radiohead were chosen as an influence by U2's Bono.

Radiohead have been enjoying renewed success with their fourth album, Kid A, eight years after the acclaimed OK Computer.

Talking about his choice, Bono commented: "I saw the new Radiohead show and it was beautiful, so beautiful, perfect sound, beautiful songs, and when he sings it breaks my heart."

Among the poll's more curious nominees was controversial US rapper Eminem.

He came in at number eight, even though his UK chart career stretches back just 19 months.

He was nominated by Happy Mondays and Black Grape singer Shaun Ryder.

British guitarist and singer Nick Drake also made it in at number nine, 26 years after his death.


Dutt's the way  
 Posted by  bhavin   bhavin@123ofnet.com

Our films may have been attacked for being formula-driven and cliches-ridden. Some phenomena however defy all norms of commercial Hindi cinema. Sanjay Dutt's meteoric rise as a dancing star not so long ago, at what was considered the fag end of his career, is just one of them.

Sanjay has never done a decent romantic role since Saajan way back in 1990. In his resurgence as one of the most dependable stars in the unpredictable box office game, he has consistently excelled in the action and comic genre, especially with films like Vaastav, Daag The Fire, Khoobsurat, Haseena Maan Jayegi and, of late, Mission Kashmir and Kurukshetra. Two genre that are never sold on song-and-dance sequences.

But wait, the 40-plus actor's track record suggests otherwise. One after the other, Sanjay's jigs have set the big screen afire. Some extremely tacky songs have gone on to become big hits, and those that haven't made it big on the music charts have got whistles in the theatres - enough to keep the distributors happy.
There have been many others. Aaila re in Jung and Oh gori tu chali kahan in Khauff were both shoved into scripts that had no rhyme or reason to them. Then you could see Sunju enjoying thoroughly in Sanjay Chhel's Khoobsurat, with Ae Shivani (he flexed his vocal chords for this one) and Ghoonghat mein chand hoga. Ditto with Maaf karo in Vidhu Vinod Chopra's Mission Kashmir, where even the tension of the hidden bomb couldn't mollify the applause Sunju's breaking into a jig got.

How does he manage it? Frankly, he is no better a dancer than Bobby Deol, certainly not as good as Akshay Kumar. But the response has been consistent enough to put both of them, as also everyone else, with the exception of perhaps a Hrithik Roshan, back into their places. Those who know Sanjay well think his simplicity and dedication might well be the reason. The honesty in his effort does get across. Others think it has to do with his immense sex appeal.



Gajagamini to see the light of the day  
 Posted by  bhavin   bhavin@123ofnet.com

After doing the rounds of film festivals around the world, M F Hussain's Gajagamini is finally nearing its release in India. The title song currently being aired on music channels has the Bhupen Hazarika stamp all over it. Bhupen has given us some distinguished film soundtracks in the past, including the haunting score for Kalpna Lajmi's Rudaali. Nothing unexpected about the artist choosing the maestro to score the music for his film. Gajagamini illustrates four different facets of womanhood, portrayed by Madhuri in four different roles. Apparently, the film is loaded with metaphors and symbolism, and the song-and-dance sequences have been designed to convey the same. While Hussain's preference for Bhupen might indicate that he is not making the film from the 'commercial' point of view, Shah Rukh Khan's special appearance song with Madhuri suggests otherwise. Gajagamini is not the only Bhupen Hazarika film awaiting release. The maestro has scored the music for Kalpana Lajmi's due-for-release third film, Daman, just as he did for her first two films, Rudaali and Darmiyaan.

Zubeidaa (OST)  
 Posted by  bhavin   bhavin@123ofnet.com

Zubeidaa is right now the hottest thing in Bollywood. Two stalwarts of cinema, i.e. AR Rahman and Shyam Benegal have joined forces for the first time. A promising combination as everyone would expect it to be. AR Rahman has always been more of a sound expert than a music director. His precision over sound has always been great. Zubeidaa is no different in that respect although the story of the movie demanded the music to be more lyrically and traditionally inclined. Javed Akhtar has given the lyrics and done a good job of it even though his poetry depends a lot on similes.

Unlike Roja, Rangeela or Bombay, music of Zubeidaa doesn't have hardcore fusion of east meeting the west. It doesn't mean that Rahman has not done a good job, its just that the story demanded more Indian sounds. Since the story is based on the life of a princess, the soundtrack has female playback singers in the forefront. Kavita Krishnamurthy has beautifully sung Dheemi Dheemi, which is probably the best track in the whole album. Sukhwinder Singh joins Kavita in Mein Albeli, which is the only track to have Rahman's trademark sound arrangement along with powerful vocal harmonies. Lata Mangeshkar again makes her presence felt on two tracks, So Gaye Hain and Pyaara sa Gaon. Although Lata sounds at her best in So Gaye Hain, it seems as if the nightingale of India is aging while singing Pyaara sa Gaon. Hai Na is a lovely duet sung by Udit Narayan and Alka Yagnik. Mehndi Hai Rachnewali is a tough song sung well by Alka Yagnik, who until recently was not much favoured by Rahman. A new voice, Richa Sharma is introduced by Rahman in Chhodo More Baiyyan. The débutante has done a fabulous job in executing a tough and a classically based Thumri.

Over all the music has captured the mood of the story quite well. Javed Akhtar's lyrics are yet again powerful gelling well with the sound. Though in its entirety, the soundtrack is not vintage Rahman its certainly better than its current competitors in Bollywood. A must buy for all Rahman fans.

Beatlemania gets a new ticket to ride  
 Posted by  bhavin   bhavin@123ofnet.com

THIRTY years after their breakup, the Beatles are reliving all their yesterdays by releasing a compilation album of their 27 Number One hits and launching their first website.

London’s HMV record store is opening especially at midnight on Sunday and awarding the first 350 visitors a certificate for their devotion to the 20th century’s biggest pop group.

Back in 1962, Brian Epstein used the old HMV store to make a demo disc by a new band he was managing — The Beatles. Now, fans in the 21st century will be able to enjoy the Beatles with an armchair ride around their songs in the electronic age.

Beginning November 13, Net surfers can ride their mice to www.thebeatles. com and see the songs on the new compilation brought to life by top internet designers given artistic help and guidance by the surviving Threatles and John Lennon’s widow, Yoko Ono.

The Beatles producer, George Martin, sometimes dubbed 'the fifth Beatle', said: "It barely took eight years to cover this collection — Love Me Do to The Long And Winding Road — but it was a lifetime in the world of rock and roll."

The timing of the launch of the site and new album is perfect for the Beatles’ record company, Britain’s EMI Group, which is currently in merger talks with BMG - the music arm of Germany’s Bertelsmann - to form the world’s leading player in the industry.

Looking forward to an international boost in sales, EMI Recorded Music CEO Ken Berry said: "The Beatles are the most influential band in popular music and no other band has had their degree of chart success."

Diehard fans have set up thousands of Beatle-related internet sites, but the group’s survivors decided it was time to take to the information superhighway on their own behalf. They hired top site designers — from Capetown to Paris, from San Francisco to London — to come up with 21st-century Song-sites to illustrate the sixties' stars.

Abbey Road Studios, giving the world’s media a sneak preview of the website designs, left it to the Beatles to write their own epitaph for a group that shows no signs of fading away.

Ringo Starr recalled: "It felt with us four it was magical and it was telepathy." George Harrison said they could never split up: "The music is there. Whatever we did is still there and always will be."

Paul McCartney said: "The Beatles were always a great little band. Nothing more, nothing less." The typically blunt and boastful words of the late John Lennon were recalled: "The Beatles really could play music together. I thought we were the best group in the goddamn world." - Reuters

The Complete Bhupen Hazarika  
 Posted by  bhavin   bhavin@123ofnet.com

The name misled me. I expected a collection of songs composed and rendered by the Assamese doctorate in his sonorous, bell-like voice that emerges from the heart of our mountains in the northeast. However, once you get over the initial disappointment, you find that the album has a lot to offer anyway.

The album brings you ten songs, composed by Bhupen Hazarika, written largely by Gulzar, and sung by modern singers like Kavita Krishnamurthy, Hariharan, Shaan, Mahalaxmi and Suneeta Rao. Bhupen Hazarika sings the introduction to each song, which strikes you as a breath of fresh, natural air before you enter an air-conditioned room.

The album incorporates Hazarika's two film songs – Duniya parayee log yahan begane from Darmiyaan and the well-known number Dil hoom hoom kare from Rudali. The clarity and the energy of Bhupen Hazarika’s renderings cannot cease to buoy the heart.

The album opens with Hazarika’s famous song Ganga tum bahati ho kyon, rendered by him and a host of other singers. It still remains one of the most stirring musical expressions of anger and dissatisfaction at the corruption, illiteracy, poverty and other ills plaguing our society, the rot that we choose to connive. In the clouds of Indipop, which float across the musical sky these days, voices of dissent, concern and anger at the injustices of our society are so rarely heard.

The song Ganga reminds you that there is still a world of problems that need to be encountered and there is no room for complacency. We need to get up and act and now. One may not grasp the precise meaning of the lyrics of the song, closely transcripted by Pt Narendra Sharma, but the music conveys the message pretty well. The music is the message.

All the songs convey universal emotions and retain their strong folk, pahadi flavour. (Kaise yeh shaher hai alone stands out as an out and out urban sophisticated number.) The poetry of the songs is fittingly rich in natural imagery. Like the song Dug dug dug dug dumbaroo, megh bajaye dumbaroo… pag mein bandhe ghungroo crooned by Shaan, Suneeta Rao and chorus, beautifully evokes the feeling of monsoons and anticipation of romance. A great composer, Hazarika uses instruments like violin and the flute as deftly as human voices in his orchestration.

It’s true, translations undermine the value of songs sometimes. Like the Hariharan number Tum hi tum loses the lyrical impact of its Bengali counterpart Manush manusheri janye, jeeban jevaner hi janye.ektu sahanubhuti ki manush dite pare na bandhu, meaning ‘Man lives for man, life is for life, can’t man show a little compassion.’

Gulzar’s lyrics - Tum hi tum ho nigahon mein, tum hi dil ki sada mein gives the song a romantic hue and detracts from the original appeal of the song.

However, in spite of some loopholes, this is an album worth listening to again and again for experiencing Bhupen Hazarika enrich one’s life.
